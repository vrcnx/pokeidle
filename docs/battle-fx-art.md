# Battle effect art

Six of the effect sprites in `game/public/fx/` are **ours**, generated rather
than vendored from Pokémon Showdown. This is how to regenerate them.

## Why they were replaced

Showdown's fx set is CC0 and comprehensive, and its *animations* are excellent
— the timing, arcs and staggering ported in `data/moveAnims.ts` are theirs and
are the reason the moves read as moves at all. But eleven of the most-used
sprites in it are soft radial gradients: `wisp`, `shadowball`, `energyball`,
`electroball`, `mistball`, `iceball`, `flareball` and friends are literally
fuzzy coloured circles with no shape, edge or texture.

That is fine on Showdown's small, flat, dark stage. On this game's detailed
painted backgrounds they read as smudges. It was the ceiling the animations
kept hitting after the timing, the opacity and the blending had each been
fixed in turn — Ice Beam's densest frame rendered as a faint haze because its
sprite was a pale blue blob on a lit cave wall.

The six replaced are the ones that carry the most moves.

## Regenerating

1. Generate a 3×2 sheet with this prompt (Nano Banana Pro, 3:2, 2K, PNG):

   > A set of 2D game visual-effect sprites for a Pokémon-style pixel-art
   > battle scene, arranged as a 3x2 grid on a pure black background, each
   > cell containing one effect centred with generous empty margin: (1) a
   > bright orange-and-yellow flame burst, (2) a crackling yellow electric orb
   > with jagged arcs, (3) a pale cyan ice crystal shard cluster, (4) a vivid
   > green energy sphere with an inner glow, (5) a dark purple ghostly shadow
   > orb with wispy edges, (6) a swirling magenta psychic sphere. Chunky
   > 16-bit pixel art, hard-edged pixels, limited saturated palette, strong
   > dark outlines, high contrast, readable at small size, no text, no
   > characters, no background scenery.

   The grid order matters — `scripts/slice-fx-sheet.mjs` maps cells to sprite
   names by position. The model tends to return a white background whatever
   the prompt asks for; that is fine, the slicer keys off the corners.

2. Slice it:

   ```bash
   node scripts/slice-fx-sheet.mjs path/to/sheet.png
   ```

   The slicer flood-fills transparency **inward from each cell's corners**
   rather than keying every pixel of the background colour — a plain colour
   key punches holes through the white highlight at the heart of the flame
   and the glints inside the ice. It then trims to the ink and rescales with
   smoothing off to the display size already declared in
   `src/data/battleFxSprites.ts`, so nothing downstream changes.

3. Look at them on a real background before committing. `public/fx/` renders
   fine on a swatch and badly on a cave.

## Licensing

These six are generated for this project and carry no third-party licence.
Everything else in `public/fx/` is Showdown's CC0 set — see
`game/public/fx/PROVENANCE.md`, which is generated by `scripts/pull-fx.mjs`
and records what was deliberately not vendored and why.

Note that re-running `pull-fx.mjs` **overwrites these six**, because it pulls
the whole vendored list. Re-run the slicer afterwards.
